# Which panel to choose?

{% hint style="success" %}
The main thing up front: **no public panel will save you from bans.** All the developers' claims about "safe farming" are marketing. Safe farming doesn't exist. Choose by convenience and features, not by promises.
{% endhint %}

<h2 align="center">💼 <mark style="color:green;">What I use — DM Panel</mark></h2>

#### After catching a few waves I settled on [**DM Panel**](https://t.me/dmpanelsbot?start=6810487864) and still don't regret it. The reasons are concrete, no fluff:

### <mark style="color:blue;">1️⃣ No need to pay for the second level</mark>

#### To start farming you don't have to buy extra games just to get the second rank. That's an immediate cut in costs on every account, and at scale the difference gets serious.

### <mark style="color:blue;">2️⃣ It eats any accounts</mark>

#### No problems finding games and no fussiness about account history. That's critical for me, because my accounts come in all kinds, and a panel that can't digest half of them doesn't work for me.

### <mark style="color:blue;">3️⃣ Global 2v2 search implemented</mark>

#### This, in my view, is the most important part. The search runs **between the accounts of all the panel's users**, not within your own batch. I tested it personally — shared games are found even on accounts with a messy history.

{% hint style="info" %}
For now hardly anyone goes into this mode — people are used to DM, and that's fine. But if something goes wrong with DM, the crowd will flow there, a community will form and the mode will come alive. I think global search is the future.
{% endhint %}

<h2 align="center">☠️ <mark style="color:green;">Why I think "draw" 2v2 is dead</mark></h2>

#### The logic is right on the surface. When thousands of accounts play each other and matches consistently end the same way — that's a pattern you can see with the naked eye. A random score didn't save it. Two different game modes didn't save it either.

#### And the latest waves got more surgical: they used to mow everything down to zero, now they leave some fresh accounts untouched entirely. That's not kindness — it's targeted work on patterns. And the more obvious your panel's pattern is, the higher the chance of getting caught.

{% hint style="warning" %}
From this a simple conclusion follows: the closer a farm's behavior is to true randomness, the longer it lives. Panels moving toward global search between real users will win in the long run over those spinning matches inside a single batch.
{% endhint %}

<h2 align="center">✅ <mark style="color:green;">Bottom line: what criteria to choose by</mark></h2>

1. #### **Subscription price** — and how it scales to your volume.
2. #### **Account requirements** — do you need a second level, do you need paid games.
3. #### **Modes** — what the panel can do and whether you'll be stuck if one mode gets shut down.
4. #### **Automation** — auto-collect and auto-send of the drop are mandatory; without them you'll be living in the panel by hand.
5. #### **Stats** — without decent numbers you can't calculate payback.
6. #### **Support** — how fast they respond when everything grinds to a halt on a Sunday night.

#### **You farm with whatever you want.** I only write about my own experience — it's all at your own risk.
